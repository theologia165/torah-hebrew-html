# 035 research sources — Genesis 25:12–18

Primary and traditional sources consulted for the researched commentary:

- Hebrew Bible / WLC, Genesis 25:12–18; cross-references Genesis 16:12; 17:20; 21:18; 25:8; 25:19; Judges 7:12; Isaiah 60:7; Ezekiel 27:21.
- Rashi on Genesis 25:13, 25:17, 25:18, via Sefaria / Chabad. Rashi reads `לְתוֹלְדֹתָם` as birth order and explains `נָפָל` in 25:18 first as `שָׁכַן`, while also transmitting the Genesis Rabbah contrast between 16:12 and 25:18.
- Sefaria, Genesis 25 with Rashi: https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.25?with=Rashi
- Chabad, Chayei Sarah 7th portion, Genesis 25:12–18: https://www.chabad.org/dailystudy/torahreading.asp
- TheTorah.com, “Reading Biblical Genealogies”: https://www.thetorah.com/article/reading-biblical-genealogies — segmented genealogies and their literary role in Genesis.
- TheTorah.com, “Ishmael, King of the Arabs”: https://www.thetorah.com/article/ishmael-king-of-the-arabs — Ishmael in the Abraham cycle and the geographical/ethnographic function of Genesis 25:12–18.
- David F. Graf, discussion of Nabaioth/Nabataeans in *Rome and the Arabian Frontier*; modern scholarship generally rejects a simple identification of biblical/Assyrian Nabayat with the later Nabataeans on linguistic and chronological grounds.
- Shuaib, M., *The Arabs of North Arabia in Later Pre-Islamic Times: Qedar, Nebaioth, and Others* (University of Manchester PhD, 2014), especially the use of Neo-Assyrian evidence for Qedar and Nebaioth.
- Detlef Jericke, “Kedar,” Ortsangaben im Buch Genesis / bibelwissenschaft.de — Qedar in biblical and Neo-Assyrian sources and its north-Arabian setting.

Research cautions:

1. Tribal names in Genesis 25 should not automatically be equated with later ethnic or political entities without independent evidence.
2. The interpretation of `נָפָל` in Genesis 25:18 as “settled/camped” is lexically plausible in context and supported by comparison with Judges 7:12 and the parallel wording of Genesis 16:12; Rashi’s further historical/midrashic contrast is distinguished from the lexical sense.
3. The geographical summary in Genesis 25:18 is treated as an ancient broad-range description, not a precise modern boundary map.
